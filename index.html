Better decisions on when to turn
the pumps on and off.

Climate change makes the weather more extreme. AGV no longer only wants to get as much water away as possible: in warm, dry weather it also wants to retain water. We built two layers that work together, for all 43 polder pumping stations.

Layer 1 · the modelPumps the way AGV's operators do A machine-learning model, trained on 26 years of AGV pump data and the weather, that does what the operators already do, by machine.

Layer 2 · the rulesDecides when to deviate Rules on top read the 14-day forecast: pump extra before heavy rain, pump less to retain water before a dry spell.

The idea in one minute
Layer 1 · the model

A machine that pumps the way AGV's operators do.

1Learned from AGV's own operationTrained on 26 years (1996–2021) of hourly pump data from 43 stations, together with the real weather.
2Tested on years it never sawOn 2022–2025 it matches the daily pumping of all stations together (R² 0.84), and per station week by week (R² 0.93).
3One model, every stationIt knows each polder's size, soil and land use, so it can say for any day: this is what AGV would usually pump.

That "usual" pumping is the baseline. The rules in layer 2 decide when, and how much, to deviate from it.

Layer 2 · the rules on top

Pump ahead of the rain, retain water ahead of the drought.

Heavy rain comingPre-pumpWhen heavy rain is forecast, we pump extra beforehand so the polder has room to take it.
Dry spell comingRetainWhen a dry spell is forecast, we pump less and keep water in the polder for later.
After a dry monthKeep the bufferAfter a dry period, a bit of rain doesn't make us pump the saved water away.
AlwaysStay within safe limitsWater levels and pumps always stay within the limits they're known to handle.

According to rough estimates, if AGV had used our system in these periods:

−22%lower peak pumping in the September 2013 storm than AGV actually pumped
1.2 million m³more water held in the polders at the driest point of spring 2023 than with usual pumping
0.9 million m³more water held at the driest point of the 2018 drought than with usual pumping

Replays of the real weather with a noisy forecast. The data has no water levels, so levels are calculated: water not pumped stays in the polder's open water (ditches and lakes, from the TOP10NL map).

Limitations · next steps

The rules are our assumptions. AGV's experience should set them.

LimitationThe rules are not validated yetThe thresholds on the previous slide (15 mm, −20 mm, 70% of the band) are our own choices. Water-board professionals need to check them, and they need testing with more data and real experiments.
LimitationNo water levels in the dataThe data has pump volumes but no level sensors, so the polder levels are simulated from pumped volume and open-water area. Letting water in during droughts is not in the data either.
Where the model is weakerSingle days at some stationsPer station, daily accuracy ranges from R² 0.28 to 0.82; week by week it is much better (0.93). And a forecast beyond about a week is uncertain, so the rules act carefully on it.
Next stepEasy to improve with AGV's knowledge The rules are a handful of readable numbers in one place. New data or experiments plug straight in: tune a rule with the operators, replay past years to test it, then trial it in manual mode at a few stations. The goal is smooth operation that anticipates the weather, without pumping water out only to let it back in. Finding the best rules takes experience, and that is not in this dataset or this hackathon.
How it meets the four criteria

Built for AGV's real problem, ready to use.

Problem fitWhen to pump, and when to retainAGV's question: optimise when the pumps go on and off, now that climate change means not only draining water, but sometimes keeping it in. The rules answer exactly that, for all 43 stations.
Technical qualityOur own model, honestly testedTrained on AGV's own data, checked on years it never saw. It matches how operators pump; the rules on top are simple and explainable.
ImpactFewer floods, less droughtPumping ahead of rain lowers the peak load on the shared main water; in dry spells the polders keep their water.
FeasibilityWorks with what AGV already hasExisting pump records and free weather forecasts, in a browser. Operators stay in charge: auto or manual mode.
